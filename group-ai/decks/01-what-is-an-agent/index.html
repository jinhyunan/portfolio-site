Judgment is probabilistic; execution is deterministic.
The wiring that connects them while keeping them distinct is the harness.

When to provide tools, what to add or remove from context, how to map procedures as a graph, when to stop — this design is what Claude Code and Codex did well

09 / 13

Break work into pieces you can delegate

Do not hand over the entire workflow at once.
Split the procedure and decide which pieces to delegate.

  • One role gathers materials, another drafts, another scrutinizes the draft.
    It is like assembling a human team.
  • The number of stages is not the number of agents. One agent can handle several stages.
    Separate agents only when roles differ.
  • Start with the simplest setup and add autonomy only when needed.

Ask “Which piece of my work can I delegate first?” rather than “Can AI do all of my work?”

Trained on our decision history, it expresses our criteria as probabilities that a commercial LLM does not know. Domain specialization comes through tools.

12 / 13

What humans still decide after delegation

  • What to delegate, and how far — humans decide.
  • What counts as done, and where to stop for approval — humans decide.

Delegating does not mean letting go.
It means designing the limits of delegation.

13 / 13

Three-line recap

  • An agent is an LLM system that repeats “goal → tool → observation → reassessment.”
    You delegate a task, beyond giving instructions.
  • The model makes judgments, tools calculate and retrieve, and people hold responsibility.
    Domain specialization also comes through tools.
  • Start with the simplest setup, and expand autonomy only as trust grows.
