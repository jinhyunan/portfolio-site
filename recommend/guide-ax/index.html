Explanation
Comparative ratings in seven areas. Rate the applicant relative to other graduate school candidates you have known.
Avoid giving the highest rating in every area

If every rating is Top 5%, reviewers may see the letter as offering little differentiation. Varying the ratings makes them read as judgments based on observation. The choices below are suggestions; please adjust them to reflect your experience.

Intellectual Ability — This is the strongest point to emphasize in the application. The central argument is that someone without a computing degree taught himself to design agent systems; a low rating here would weaken that argument.

Writing Ability — He writes English technical documents and public case studies himself. However, his English is not at a native-speaker level, so the very highest rating could seem overstated. One level lower is more credible.

Speaking Ability — There is no need to give a high rating if you have rarely seen him present. His experience presenting in English is also limited, so this rating is closer to the evidence.

Academic Preparation — His undergraduate background is mechanical and electronic control engineering, and he has been out of university for 12 years. The highest rating could prompt questions when compared with his transcript. Describe his completed company training and current GTx and MIT courses to provide context.

Motivation — After 12 years in industry, he built a 4,200-line calculation engine and added 223 tests outside working hours. This is the best-supported category, making the highest rating a natural choice.

Maturity — His 12 years in multinational EPC work provide the evidence. However, this category offers limited differentiation, so the highest rating adds relatively little.

Teaching Ability — The AX/PI Group manages resources and adoption, so you may not have observed him teaching. N/A is appropriate if you have not directly seen him teach. Rating an area you did not observe can also weaken the credibility of the other ratings.

Explanation
Submission

You do not need to finish in one sitting. Save your progress and return later.

After submission, the applicant sees only whether the recommendation was submitted. Because the applicant has waived FERPA access, the applicant cannot view what you wrote.

To complete the form. Select the seven ratings and overall recommendation, enter your relationship and how long you have known the applicant, then upload the letter. The suggested ratings have supporting reasons for each category. If your observations differ, your assessment takes precedence.
